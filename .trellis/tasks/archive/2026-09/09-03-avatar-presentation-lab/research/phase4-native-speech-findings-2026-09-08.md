# Phase 4 native speech findings

## Decision and scope

The existing engine plays real speech and moves the mouth in both tested model generations. Natural pauses
produce zero mouth opening; the earlier assumption that native playback necessarily leaves the mouth ajar is
not supported. The first useful comparison is native gain tuning. Custom envelopes, inserted closures, fake
mouth control and mood/expression relax remain separate future decisions.

All new runtime code is under `apps/desktop/lab/`; the engine and production renderer/Core are unchanged.
Native blink/sway were already accepted by the owner for proceeding. No additional implementation was added for
them. Fish Audio and the reserved OpenAI-compatible adapters are implemented, but live provider acceptance is pending.

## Native measurements

The following values describe samples from this fixture, not a universal perceptual quality score. The native
weight stayed at 0.4; the baseline gain stayed at its engine default of 1.5. Gain=1 was an explicit lab-only control.

| Model and gain | Positive / zero analysed frames | Positive samples at computed cap | Actual final mouth range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C2, gain 1.5 | 238 / 250 | 192 / 238 (80.7%) | 0 to 0.4000 |
| C3, gain 1.5 | 63 / 57 | 54 / 63 (85.7%) | 0 to 0.4000 |
| C2, gain 1.0 | 234 / 254 | 2 / 234 (0.9%) | 0 to 0.4000 |
| C3, gain 1.0 | 44 / 37 | 1 / 44 (2.3%) | 0 to 0.3544 |

Both defaults and gain=1 returned to zero during observed pauses. Gain=1 substantially reduced saturation while
using the same engine and the same retained audio. Keep the default unchanged until target-voice observations
justify tuning; compare gain/weight using the lab controls before proposing another mouth driver.

The monitor reads the analyser after the engine's own lip-sync step. Its computed value is a formula applied to
that observation, not instrumentation of the engine's exact earlier read. Audio quanta, later model writes and
render timing can make the computed contribution differ from the final parameter. Both are retained separately.

## Engine and lifecycle facts

- `speak()` creates audio and resolves initialization success. Browser playback is checked through the actual
  `currentAudio.isPlaying` and AudioContext state; no acknowledgement is treated as audible output proof.
- `SoundManager.analyze()` computes RMS, converts to decibels, normalizes against the analyser's configured range
  and rounds to tenths. The render path applies gain, power 1.15, a positive-only floor of 0.1, cap 1 and weight 0.4.
  Zero stays zero. The default positive minimum contribution is 0.04, not an unconditional mouth-open floor.
- The analyser has a separate decoded-buffer source from the audible player. Observation start differences include
  the fixture's leading silence and browser scheduling; they do not measure physical speaker latency or certify A/V sync.
- Native finish/Stop clears `currentAudio` but can retain `currentAnalyzer`/`currentContext` references. The lab
  reports those references and makes raw/contribution unavailable without current audio. It does not falsify cleanup
  by assigning zero values or modifying the engine's internals.
- Native loading cannot be aborted. Stop/replace/dispose retires the current run, retains a pending-load lock,
  and stops the captured old manager when the promise settles. New playback cannot overlap that retirement.
- Every play owns a new object URL. Stop/finish/failure/dispose releases it after any pending native load settles;
  retained clip bytes can be replayed without another synthesis request.
- Source replacement advances the observation generation and clears timing immediately, including while the
  ticker is paused. Independent review reproduced stale A timestamps on unplayed B; unit and browser regressions
  now cover that path and late A completion cannot republish B's timing.
